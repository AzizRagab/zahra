Model Answer 2 / 2

The four options Avoid: eliminate the threat by eliminating its cause, e.g. removing a troublesome resource or cancelling a high-risk activity. Mitigate: reduce the probability or the consequences of the risk, e.g. training the team in conflict resolution or adding controls. Accept: take no action and notify management of the exposure, appropriate when the cost of treatment exceeds the benefit. Owner and action plan Each risk is assigned to an owner who develops and executes the response and may act on predetermined triggers, while the risk action plan makes the agreed response cost effective and continuously monitored for residual risk. Essay 1 - Model Answer Access management remediation programme (25 marks) (a) Policy: issue a topic-specific access control policy approved by management, applying need-to-know (data) and need-to-use (systems and functions) so entitlements follow business role, not convenience. (b) Identity life-cycle (5.16): unique identity per person; joiner-mover-leaver triggers; shared and non-human identities only with documented business justification, dedicated approval, named owner and independent ongoing oversight. (c) Authentication information (5.17): secure allocation channels, no clear-text distribution, forced change at first use, identity verification before issue, and a password system that blocks common and breached credentials and stores secrets protected. (d) Rights (5.18): formal request, authorization by the asset owner, provisioning separate from approval, periodic recertification, and immediate revocation on role change or exit, with segregation of duties enforced across request, approve and implement. Essay 2 - Model Answer From incident to lasting ISMS improvement (25 marks) (a) Four stages: detection and reporting by any employee; initial assessment and decision by the operational support group; second evaluation and confirmation by the CSIRT with severity assessment; response, restoration, record completion, verification of actions and formal closure. Records must capture unique identifier, reporter, date and time, category and priority, symptoms, affected assets, actions taken, evidence, and closure approval. (b) Forensics: admissible evidence needs documented, repeatable and defensible procedures for identification, collection, acquisition and preservation, with chain of custody and integrity protection. Under control 5.28 capability may be internal or supplied by external specialists - decide on incident volume, in-house competence, legal exposure and cost, and contract external support in advance. (c) Audit and nonconformity: findings from the internal audit become nonconformities requiring root-cause analysis and correction; audit results, incident and nonconformity status, and performance measurement feed the management review inputs, whose outputs are improvement decisions, ISMS changes and resource allocation. (d) Corrective action removes the cause of an actual nonconformity; preventive action addresses potential causes through risk treatment. Effectiveness asks whether the intended result was achieved; efficiency asks at what cost of resources - both must be evaluated. (e) Continual improvement is evidenced by defined measurement objectives, indicators tied to those objectives, a stated monitoring frequency, and trend analysis showing recurrence falling and response times improving over successive review cycles.
